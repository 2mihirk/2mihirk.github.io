Ant Colony Foraging Simulation

Modeled how simple local behavior can produce efficient colony-level foraging without a central planner.

← Back to projects
Goal

Model colony behavior from local rules

Develop a MATLAB simulation that models how an ant colony searches for food and coordinates food collection using local sensing and pheromone trails. The system needed to support exploration, food detection, pheromone-based navigation, colony return, boundary constraints, and pheromone decay without centralized control.

My Contribution

Simulation architecture & algorithm tuning

Developed the simulation architecture and implemented the core ant behaviors in MATLAB, including pheromone detection, concentration-weighted direction selection, randomized exploration, movement validation, food and colony proximity detection, and pheromone decay.

Represented ants, food sources, and pheromones as dynamic arrays and integrated the individual functions into a time-stepped simulation. Tuned the smell radius, exploration parameters, and pheromone decay rates through repeated testing to improve food-collection performance across different maps.

Result

The final simulation produced coordinated colony behavior through local rules, with the colony collecting 93 units of food on Map 1 and 259 units on Map 2. Testing also exposed a limitation where ants could become trapped following one another, providing a clear direction for future algorithm improvements. The project strengthened my experience with MATLAB, algorithm development, simulation architecture, parameter optimization, and debugging emergent behavior.
